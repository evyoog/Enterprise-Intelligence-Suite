# Business rules — SLA management (REQ-SUP-002)

Decided in [C101](../../../01-business/roadmap/open-decisions.md#c101) (SL-1 B, SL-2 B, 2026-10-14). Enforced by the backend; the cited class and method are the source of truth.

| ID | Rule | Enforced in | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| BR-SLA-001 | **One policy per priority**, the same for every customer and plan: first-response minutes, resolution minutes, around-the-clock flag. Seeded: URGENT 60 / 480 minutes, around the clock; HIGH 240 / 480; MEDIUM 480 / 1440; LOW 480 / 2400, business hours. No per-plan or per-customer policy exists. | database (seed), `SlaService#policy` | .1, .2 |
| BR-SLA-002 | **Business hours** are one settings row: working days (seeded `MON,TUE,WED,THU,FRI`), start 09:00, end 18:00, time zone `Asia/Kolkata`, warning point 80%, alert emails. A target of *n* minutes counts *n* minutes inside the business window on working days; the next minute after closing is the next working day's opening. **1 day = 8 business hours = 480 minutes** (the targets are stored in minutes). Public holidays are not modelled. | `SlaCalculator`, `SlaSettings` | .3 |
| BR-SLA-003 | **Around the clock** (Urgent by default) adds the minutes to the instant as they are: nights and weekends count. | `SlaCalculator#add` | .3 |
| BR-SLA-004 | **The clock starts at creation** (`createdAt`) and **never pauses**, for any status or any waiting on the customer. A ticket created outside business hours (before opening, after closing or on a non-working day) starts at the next opening. | `SlaService#applyDeadlines` | .4 |
| BR-SLA-005 | **Both deadlines are set when the ticket is created**, from `createdAt` and the priority (MEDIUM for a new ticket), and stored on the ticket (`first_response_due_at`, `resolution_due_at`). | `SupportTicketService#createTicket` | .5 |
| BR-SLA-006 | **First response** = the first time a person takes the ticket out of Open: **assigning** it (an assignee is given), **escalating** it or **resolving** it. It is stored once (`first_responded_at`) and never replaced. Categorizing or changing the priority without assigning is not a response. The automatic escalation of a breach is not a response; a person who answers afterwards records the response, late. | `SupportTicketService#markFirstResponse` | .6 |
| BR-SLA-007 | **Resolution** = the time the ticket is resolved (`resolved_at`). | `SupportTicketService#resolveTicket` | .7 |
| BR-SLA-008 | **Priority change recalculates both deadlines from the creation time** with the new priority's targets, and clears the two warning marks so a warning can go out again. A breach already recorded stays. It applies to a priority change in the update call and to an escalation (which sets URGENT). A ticket with no deadlines is not given any. | `SupportTicketService#updateTicket`, `#escalateTicket`, `#recalculateDeadlines` | .8, .14 |
| BR-SLA-009 | **Warning.** For each ticket that is not RESOLVED or CLOSED and has a resolution deadline, the check warns once per target when the warning point (default 80% of the target, counted in business time from creation, at least 1 minute) has passed and no breach is recorded: for the **first-response** target only while the ticket has no first response, for the **resolution** target until it is resolved. | `SlaService#check` | .9 |
| BR-SLA-010 | **Breach.** When the deadline has passed (`now ≥ due`) and no breach is recorded for that target, the ticket's breach mark is stored, its **status becomes ESCALATED** (if it was not already) and its **priority is unchanged**; the alert is sent and `TICKET_SLA_BREACHED` is audited. A ticket checked for the first time after its deadline goes straight to breach without a warning. A target is breached at most once. | `SlaService#check`, `#breach` | .10 |
| BR-SLA-011 | **Alert recipients.** The ticket's **assignee** (when there is one) and every valid address on the **alert list**; each address is told once per alert (the assignee's address is not told again if it is also listed). A person with an account (found by assignee, or by email address) gets an in-app notice (category SYSTEM; severity WARNING for a warning, CRITICAL for a breach) and the notification email unless muted; an address with no account gets the email only. A failed email is logged and does not stop the check. Warnings are audited (`TICKET_SLA_WARNING`). | `SlaService#alert`, `#warn`, `#breach` | .9, .10, .11 |
| BR-SLA-012 | **Check.** The scheduled job runs every minute (`app.sla.interval-ms`, first run after 30 s; `app.sla.job-enabled=false` turns it off). A RESOLVED or CLOSED ticket is no longer watched: it is never warned or breached afterwards. | `SlaCheckJob`, `SlaService#check` | .9, .10 |
| BR-SLA-013 | **State shown to administrators**, for each target, from the stored marks and `now`: no deadline = none; done on time = `MET`; done after the deadline or after a recorded breach = `MET_LATE`; not done and a breach recorded or the deadline passed = `BREACHED`; not done and warned = `WARNING`; otherwise `ON_TRACK`. A resolved ticket's first-response state is judged against its first response. | `SlaService#state`, `#firstResponseState`, `#resolutionState` | .13 |
| BR-SLA-014 | **Customers see deadlines only.** In the requester's own ticket view the deadlines and the time of the first response are returned and **the two state fields are null**; administrators get the states. | `SupportTicketService#toDto` | .13 |
| BR-SLA-015 | **Settings.** Editable with `MANAGE_SUPPORT_TICKETS` (`/admin/support/**`). Targets: 1 minute to one year (525 600 minutes); first response not longer than resolution; a known priority. Business hours: at least one day (`MON` to `SUN`, three letters or full names), a start before the end (`HH:mm`), a known time zone, a warning point from 1 to 99, at most 20 alert addresses, each a valid email address (stored in lowercase, duplicates removed). Any invalid value refuses the whole request (400). | backend | .12; `SlaService#updatePolicies`, `#updateSettings` |
| BR-SLA-016 | **New targets are not retroactive.** Saving targets or settings does not touch existing tickets; deadlines already set stay until the ticket's priority changes. | backend | .12 |
| BR-SLA-017 | **Tickets created before SLA tracking have no deadlines**: the SLA columns are null, no state is shown, the check skips them (it selects tickets with a resolution deadline) and a priority change does not create deadlines. | backend, database | .14 |
| BR-SLA-018 | **Audit.** `SLA_POLICY_UPDATED`, `SLA_SETTINGS_UPDATED`, `TICKET_SLA_WARNING`, `TICKET_SLA_BREACHED`. The ticket actions keep their own entries ([BR-SUP-007](../ticket-management/business-rules.md)). | backend | .12 |

## Worked examples (business hours Monday to Friday 09:00 to 18:00, Asia/Kolkata)
| Ticket created | Priority | Deadline | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday 10:00 | High, first response (240 min) | Monday 14:00 | Inside the same day |
| Friday 16:00 | High, first response (240 min) | Monday 11:00 | Two hours on Friday, two on Monday |
| Saturday 12:00 | High, first response (240 min) | Monday 13:00 | Counting starts at the next opening, Monday 09:00 |
| Monday 09:00 | Medium, resolution (1440 min) | Wednesday 15:00 | Two 9-hour windows (1080) and 6 hours |
| Saturday 23:30 | Urgent, first response (60 min) | Sunday 00:30 | 24x7: the clock does not stop at night |
